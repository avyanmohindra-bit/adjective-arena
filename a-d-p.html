What are Degrees of Adjectives?

An adjective describes a noun or pronoun. For example, tall, beautiful, and interesting are adjectives.

The degrees of adjectives show different levels of comparison. There are three degrees:

1. Positive Degree

It simply describes one person, place, animal, or thing. It does not compare.

Avyan is an excellent football player.

2. Comparative Degree

It compares two people, places, animals, or things.

Avyan is more focused than Darsh during the match.

3. Superlative Degree

It compares three or more and shows the highest or lowest degree.

Avyan is the most encouraging player on the team.
Class team: “Avyan is helpful and positive, Darsh is mischievous and can sometimes distract the team, and Prabhav is smart and quick-thinking.”

Topic 1

Know the Three Degrees

🏃 Easy Start

Positive describes one: “Avyan is fast on the football field.”

Comparative compares two: “Avyan is faster than Darsh in a sprint.”

Superlative compares three or more: “Avyan is the most encouraging player on the team.”

Sports sentence: “Prabhav is smart during the game.” → “Prabhav is more alert than Darsh during the drill.” → “Avyan is the most encouraging player on the field.”
Quick Check: Which degree compares two players?
Topic 2

How to Form Comparatives & Superlatives

⚽ Build the Form

For many short adjectives, add -er and -est: fast → faster → fastest.

For adjectives ending in -y, change y to i: happy → happier → happiest.

For longer adjectives, use more and most: exciting → more exciting → most exciting.

Sports sentences: “Avyan is faster than Darsh.” “This is the most exciting match of the season.” “Prabhav is happier after making a smart assist.”

Topic 3

Irregular Adjectives & Smart Usage

🏏 Level Up

Some adjectives do not follow the usual patterns. Learn them as special forms:

Positive Comparative Superlative
good better best
bad worse worst
far farther / further farthest / furthest
Sports sentences: “Avyan is better at football than Darsh.” “Prabhav made the best tactical decision of the game.” “Darsh's most distracting moment came when he started joking during the drill.”
Quick Check: Complete: “Arjun is ___ player on our basketball team.”

Important Rules

Type of adjective Positive Comparative Superlative
Short adjective tall taller tallest
Ends in -y happy happier happiest
Long adjective beautiful more beautiful most beautiful
Irregular good better best
Irregular bad worse worst
Remember: Comparative adjectives are often used with than, while superlative adjectives commonly use the.

More Examples

Fast

Positive: Aarav is fast.

Comparative: Aarav is faster than Kabir.

Superlative: Aarav is the fastest runner.

Easy

Positive: This lesson is easy.

Comparative: This lesson is easier than the last one.

Superlative: This is the easiest lesson.

Interesting

Positive: The book is interesting.

Comparative: This book is more interesting than that one.

Superlative: This is the most interesting book here.

Football: “The captain is calmer than the striker, but the goalkeeper is the calmest player on the pitch.”
Cricket: “This bowler is faster than the last bowler, and that was the fastest delivery of the match.”
Tennis: “My new serve is more powerful than my old serve.”
